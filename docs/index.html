Find any app, file or clipboard entry in a keystroke.

Lintel is a native macOS launcher with four things: app search, file search with its own index, an encrypted clipboard history that pastes straight into the app you were using, and optional meeting transcripts with notes.

Download for macOS Source on GitHub

macOS 26 or later · Apple silicon · No account, no telemetry

Four things, done fast.

Press the shortcut and type. Results come from Lintel's own in-memory indexes, so the panel opens instantly and answers as you type.

  • App search

    Fuzzy matching finds apps by any part of the name: “vsc” finds Visual Studio Code. Lintel learns which results you pick and moves them up, without ever overruling a better match.

    With an empty search, Lintel suggests the apps you use most. It records on your Mac which apps you use and how often; nothing leaves your Mac, and you can turn it off or clear it in Settings.

  • File search

    Its own index of file and folder names in your home folder and Applications, kept fresh with FSEvents instead of Spotlight. Names only: Lintel never reads what is inside your files.

  • Clipboard history

    Text, links, rich text, images and files from the last 30 days (up to 1000 entries), encrypted on disk with a key in your Keychain. Skips password managers and concealed items. Return pastes into the previous app.

  • Meeting transcripts and notes

    Optional. Record a meeting with the microphone and, if you allow it, the system audio, and get a transcript with speakers from models that run on your Mac. After the meeting a language model that also runs on your Mac can write notes in the background: a summary, decisions, action items and open questions. Every point must quote the recording, and points without a matching quote are left out. The notes can still contain mistakes. Meetings are kept in an encrypted library you can search and export as Markdown.

Native and accessible

  • Swift, AppKit and SwiftUI with the system's Liquid Glass; light and dark mode follow macOS.
  • VoiceOver labels and announcements, Reduce Motion, Reduce Transparency, Increase Contrast and a text-size setting.
  • Every feature can be turned off in Settings, and its data erased.
  • English and Polish, following your system language.

Only the permissions a feature needs.

Lintel never asks for a permission on its own. Settings → Permissions shows each one with its state and a button to System Settings. Nothing leaves your Mac.

Accessibility

Lets the clipboard history paste into the app you were using. Without it, Return copies the entry and you paste yourself.

Optional

Full Disk Access

Lets file search include Desktop, Documents, Downloads and iCloud Drive without asking about each folder. Without it, you can add those folders one at a time.

Optional

Clipboard access

macOS can ask before an app reads the clipboard. Allow Lintel in Privacy & Security so the history keeps recording.

Optional

System audio recording

Lets Lintel transcribe the other people in an online meeting. It captures all sound your Mac plays, not only the meeting app, and never Lintel's own. Without it, meetings record the microphone only.

Optional

Keychain

The encryption keys of the clipboard history and the meeting library live in your login keychain. No permission is needed; macOS may ask once to allow access.

Install in a minute.

  1. Download Lintel.dmg and open it.
  2. Drag Lintel onto the Applications folder.
  3. Open Lintel from Applications. A paw appears in the menu bar and the welcome screen walks you through the shortcut and each feature; every step can be skipped.

First launch: Gatekeeper

Lintel is signed with its own stable certificate, so macOS keeps your permissions across updates, but it is not notarized by Apple. The first time you open it, macOS blocks it. To allow it once:

  1. Open Lintel and close the warning.
  2. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and scroll down.
  3. Click Open Anyway next to the message about Lintel, then confirm with your password or Touch ID.

Or build it from source

Requires Xcode 27 (Swift 6.4).

git clone --recurse-submodules https://github.com/mjozefiak/Pounce.git
cd Lintel
swift package resolve --disable-keychain
scripts/make-app.sh    # dist/Lintel.app

Questions.

How do I use ⌘Space if Spotlight has it?
On first launch Lintel suggests ⌃⌥Space and shows how to turn off “Show Spotlight search” in System Settings → Keyboard → Keyboard Shortcuts → Spotlight. You can record any shortcut in Settings → General.
Does Lintel search inside files?
No. It matches file and folder names only, from its own index on your Mac. Folders like node_modules, .git, caches and ~/Library are left out by default.
What does the clipboard history keep?
Up to 30 days or 1000 entries, encrypted with SQLCipher. Entries copied in ignored apps (password managers by default) and items marked concealed are never recorded. You can pause recording or clear the history any time.
Does it send anything over the network?
Only when you download a speech, speaker or language model for dictation or meetings, from huggingface.co, after you click Download. Otherwise no: there is no account, no analytics and no update check. Audio, dictated text, meeting transcripts and notes never leave your Mac.
How does dictation work?
Turn on Dictation in Settings → Features, download the speech model (about 0.7 GB) and press ⌃⌥D. Lintel records while you talk, turns it into text with a model that runs on your Mac, and pastes it into the app you are in. Audio is never saved. To tidy up the text you can use a model that Lintel downloads and runs itself, or your own local server.
Does Lintel record other people in a meeting?
If you allow System Audio Recording, a meeting records all sound your Mac plays, so the voices of other people are processed too, but only on your Mac. The audio is stored encrypted until the transcript is ready, then deleted. If a meeting is interrupted, its audio is kept for up to 7 days so you can finish it, then deleted. Nothing leaves your Mac except the model download. Tell the people you record, and check the rules that apply to you.
Does Lintel watch my microphone or my apps?
Only if you turn on meeting suggestions, which are off by default. Lintel then checks which app is using the microphone (just the app and whether it is in use, never what it hears), and only to ask “Looks like a meeting in Zoom. Record?” It never starts recording by itself, shows nothing in screen sharing, and keeps only the list of apps you muted or added. For a meeting you start from a suggestion, it also stores that app’s ID with the meeting.
Why does macOS warn me when I open Lintel?
Notarization needs a paid Apple developer account, and Lintel is a free project. Allow it once with Open Anyway (see Install), or build it yourself from the source.
